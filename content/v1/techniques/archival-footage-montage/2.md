# How to make an archival footage montage that feels like a film

Make a cinematic archival footage montage with purposeful cuts, matched color and music. Includes NASA footage, reusable prompts and an editable short-film recipe.

Source: https://voyager.so/blog/archival-footage-montage

A hand adjusts a control. A pen moves across a chart. Then a rocket fills the frame.

A strong archival montage connects small details to a larger idea. For this 32-second film, ours was **“Extraordinary journeys begin with ordinary human work.”** The same approach can give a video essay, music visual or company-history film an opening that makes people want to keep watching.

## Give the edit a direction

Give your editor or AI assistant a subject, an emotional arc and constraints. Use this prompt with a tool that can inspect and edit your footage:

```text
Make a 30–35 second archival montage about human ingenuity.
Begin with hands, instruments and small acts of preparation.
Build toward a launch, then end on a quiet view of Earth.

Use real footage from the supplied source files. Keep a ledger
of filenames, source in/out times and credits. Choose adjacent
shots for a reason: related movement, shape, scale or meaning.

Keep the original 4:3 composition. Use mostly straight cuts.
Match exposure and color between clips before adding a shared
look. Preserve the existing grain. No fake scratches or flashes.
Use an instrumental score, with room for the ending to breathe.
Show the shot plan before rendering.
```

## Find footage with something to cut between

Search for actions as well as subjects: **hands operating controls, plotting a chart, machinery moving, people waiting**. Pair establishing shots with close-ups that connect to the next image.

Our sources were NASA’s [Apollo historical B-roll](https://images.nasa.gov/details/KSC-19940705-MH-NAS01-0001-Apollo_11_25th_Historical_Footage_and_Broll-B_0905), [Saturn V and crawler reel](https://images.nasa.gov/details/KSC-1960s-MH-NAS01-0001-Apollo_Saturn_V_Crawler,_VAB_Pad_Film_To_Tape), and [Mission Control film](https://images.nasa.gov/details/NDTV000910_Apollo_Digest_Series_Mission_Control). Download the best available transfer; enlarging a preview cannot restore missing detail.

Keep the source page beside each file. NASA’s [media guidelines](https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/images-and-media/) describe permitted uses, attribution and exceptions, including third-party material. Check the individual asset; a historical subject does not make every upload free to reuse. Clear the music separately, too.

## Make the shots belong together

Here is the structure of our cut:

| Time         | Picture                                 | What the cut does                                |
| ------------ | --------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| 0–5s         | A console operator, then a hand drawing | Repeats a small human gesture                    |
| 5–10s        | Rocket structure and crawler tracks     | Expands the scale of the work                    |
| 10–16.25s    | An astronaut, release arms, ignition    | Gives the launch a human stake, then accelerates |
| 16.25–23.75s | Liftoff and ascent                      | Lets the upward movement develop                 |
| 23.75–32s    | Earth above the Moon, then Earth alone  | Releases the tension and gives the ending space  |

The two hands belong together because both show people making decisions. The face before ignition makes the machinery matter. These are connections of meaning, not just matching shapes.

We shortened the cuts around ignition to 1.25 seconds and gave the final Earth shot 4.5 seconds. Let an action finish before cutting just to satisfy a beat. Scrub each selection’s first and last few frames: compilation reels can leave an unwanted glimpse of the previous shot.

## Match the footage before adding a look

Archive reels differ in color, contrast and embedded borders. One LUT will not make them match.

Choose a reference shot. Then work in this order:

1. **Normalize the frame.** Remove baked-in borders without cutting into the picture. Preserve the intended aspect ratio; check for interlacing before grading.
2. **Correct each source.** Bring exposure into a useful range and reduce distracting casts. In our console shot, the white shirt and skin were visibly magenta.
3. **Add a restrained shared finish.** Use a gentle contrast curve and modest saturation changes. Compare skin, metal and highlights across adjacent shots.

![The same console frame before grading on the left and after reducing its magenta cast on the right.](https://cdn.moda.app/voyager/blog/archival-footage-montage/grade-controls-83d2df27dbb83b7a.webp)

Left: normalized frame before color correction. Right: graded frame. The shirt becomes more neutral while the original texture remains.

Keep space dark and the launch bright. Matching does not mean equal exposure. Check existing grain before adding more, and avoid sharpening a soft transfer until its edges glitter.

## Give the music an arc, then fit it to the picture

Choose the score while the cut is flexible. We generated an instrumental with Eleven Music, using this direction:

```text
32-second instrumental space-documentary score, 96 BPM.
Warm analog sequencer, soft bass, restrained brushed percussion.
Start spare. Build gradually, open into sustained string-machine
chords around the launch, then let the percussion fall away.
Resolve before the final frame and leave time for the decay.
No vocals, trailer impacts, brass fanfare or EDM drop.
```

This is a reusable adaptation of the generation prompt. Timing instructions guide a music model; they do not place guaranteed edit points. Move a cut or adjust the cue after hearing the result. Keep the music separate from picture until the final mix.

## Take the revision further in Voyager

Reuse the same source ledger, corrected selections and score for a shorter cut:

```text
Make a 15-second version using these same graded selections.
Keep the hands → launch → Earth progression. Preserve the
source references and the existing grade. Reuse the score’s
closing passage so the shorter cut still has an ending.
Write a new output and leave the 32-second version unchanged.
```

Voyager’s assembly command takes explicit trims, framing and audio fades. Here, the agent reused the grades, preserved the source references and left the full-length film unchanged. We got another deliverable without generating more footage. You still decide whether the cut works.

For the earlier selection stage, see [how to search existing footage and build a rough cut](https://voyager.so/blog/find-existing-footage).

**Start with ten useful shots and one clear thought.** Watch once without sound: the images should still have a direction. Then listen without looking: the music should have somewhere to go. When both work, bring them together and remove anything that weakens the journey.
