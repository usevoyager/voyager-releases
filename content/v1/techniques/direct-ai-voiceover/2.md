# How to direct an AI voiceover and check pronunciation

Audition AI voices with Eleven v4, check brand-name pronunciation in the full read, and keep your script and takes organized. Includes prompts and audio examples.

Source: https://voyager.so/blog/direct-ai-voiceover

A convincing voice can still say your name wrong. Audition a real sentence, establish its pronunciation, then check the full read before using it in a product demo, workshop announcement or narrated walkthrough.

We tried [Eleven v4](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/changelog/2026/9/28) for a short invitation to **Serein**, a fictional ceramics studio. Casting was straightforward. Getting the name to stay consistent took more work.

## Audition AI voices on your own script

Voice-library samples tell you what a narrator can sound like. Your script tells you whether that narrator fits the job.

```text
Cast a warm, natural English narrator for a small ceramics workshop. Inviting, not breathy or hard-selling.

Have three distinct voices read this exact sentence with the same direction:
“At Serein, we turn a lump of clay into the cup you reach for every morning.”

Start with Natural delivery and a gentle, warm tone. Compare the pacing, the brand name and whether the invitation sounds like someone you would want to learn from.
```

We chose George's quieter, grounded delivery. Sarah sounded more commercial; Lily leaned breathier. Those are choices for this invitation, not a ranking of the voices.

## Check the name in the full read

For this invented studio, we wanted **suh-REN**, with stress on the second syllable. A respelling rule produced that stress in a short audition. The complete read brought back the first-syllable stress, despite using the same saved rule. Several sentence repairs did not give us a complete, consistently pronounced result.

Keep **Serein** in the script and captions. Put the sound-alike spelling in a pronunciation rule, then hear it in context. A correctly spelled transcript cannot tell you which syllable was stressed.

## Keep the production together in Voyager

With a media account connected, Voyager's Voice panel saves the script, cast, delivery, pronunciation rules and takes. We could change the pronunciation approach while keeping George and the written invitation.

```text
Keep George, the written script and the warm delivery. The name should sound suh-REN, with stress on REN. Check which pronunciation controls the model supports. Show what needs to be re-recorded and the cost before generating. Preserve the original so we can compare.
```

For the final take, we switched to v3 and used IPA `/səˈrɛn/` in the saved pronunciation rule. That read gave us the intended second-syllable stress and the complete invitation. Hear the comparison above; one name is not a general model ranking.

Changing models required a **whole new take**: a repair stays on its parent's model. Voyager kept the earlier takes and saved casting decisions, so we did not have to rebuild the production or overwrite the original.
