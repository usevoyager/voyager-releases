# How to sync music to video: make the reveal land on the beat

Score a short video around its reveal and logo. Use a cue sheet, a simple tempo calculation and editable MIDI to make shorter versions that still have an ending.

Source: https://voyager.so/blog/score-video

> **Note:** Voyager's built-in Design, Video and DAW apps are on by default on Mac. On the Windows alpha, turn them on in Settings first.

A soundtrack can be exactly twelve seconds long and still miss the moment. The product arrives between beats. The logo appears halfway through a phrase. The music stops because the video ran out.

**Start with the reveal and work backward.** Pick the few moments the music should acknowledge, then write the rhythm and ending around them. Here’s a twelve-second ident for a fictional listening club, followed by a shorter version that keeps its final chord.

## Mark the moments before choosing the music

Make a small cue sheet: an event, its time, and what the music should do. You don’t need an accent on every cut. Here, the important events are the record’s reveal and the final name.

| Event        | Full version | Short version | Musical action               |
| ------------ | ------------ | ------------- | ---------------------------- |
| Build begins | 2.4 s        | 2.4 s         | Add bass and percussion      |
| Reveal       | 7.2 s        | 4.8 s         | Bring in the full chord      |
| Name appears | 9.6 s        | 7.2 s         | Play the final chord         |
| Picture ends | 12 s         | 9.6 s         | Let the existing tail finish |

Keep picture markers fixed in seconds while you try tempos. Otherwise, changing the tempo can move the target you’re trying to hit. In Logic Pro, for example, movie scene markers stay at their absolute time when the tempo changes. [Apple’s scene-marker guide](https://support.apple.com/en-ie/guide/logicpro/lgcpfff75e20/mac).

## Find a tempo that fits the reveal

At **100 BPM**, one beat lasts **0.6 seconds**. In 4/4, four beats make a **2.4-second bar**. That puts our reveal on the start of bar four and the name on bar five, with a full bar left for the ending.

For your own cut, try:

**BPM = beats before the hit × 60 ÷ seconds before the hit.**

Twelve beats before a reveal at 7.2 seconds gives `12 × 60 ÷ 7.2 = 100`. If the result is too fast or slow for the piece, try a different number of beats. If the reveal and logo cannot both fit one tempo, prioritize the more important hit or use a tempo change. Don’t distort the whole edit to acknowledge an incidental cut.

Use this brief with a composer, a music agent or your own DAW. Replace the timings before reusing it:

```text
Score this 12-second video in D minor at 100 BPM.

Use electric-piano-style keys, muted bass, small dry percussion
and a short D–F–A motif. No vocals.

Build from 2.4 seconds. Thin the arrangement before the reveal
at 7.2 seconds, then bring the chord and bass back together.
Put the final chord at 9.6 seconds when the name appears.
Leave its ring-out intact until the picture ends at 12 seconds.

Keep the notes and instruments editable. Export the mix,
separate instrument stems and MIDI.
```

Our example uses original notes played by Voyager DAW’s built-in synthesizers. The [download includes the MIDI, stems and editable projects](https://cdn.moda.app/voyager/blog/score-video/off-the-clock-kit-4418b5cc7d2e203b.zip), so you can change the instruments or arrangement in your own tools. MIDI carries notes and timing; assign your own sounds when importing it into another DAW.

## Carry the edit into Voyager’s score

Voyager’s useful contribution here is the handoff between its video and music tools. Its Video engine exports the edit’s markers and timing as a cue sheet. The DAW tempo solver reads that file and checks candidate tempos against those events.

For this cut, searching 90–110 BPM returned 100 BPM with zero timing error at the marked build, reveal and ending. The agent then composed the separate keys, bass, kick, rim and lead tracks around that grid. The final video was assembled in Voyager Video; the artwork remains editable in Voyager Design.

## Shorten the middle, keep the ending

For the 9.6-second version, we removed one bar from the middle of the picture and one build bar from the score. The reveal and final chord move earlier together. The last 2.4 seconds still belong to the ending.

```text
Make a 9.6-second version by removing one build bar.
Keep the opening, reveal and complete ending.

Recheck the picture markers: reveal at 4.8 seconds,
name at 7.2 seconds, end at 9.6 seconds.
Export a fresh cue sheet and check the musical grid against it.
Keep the same motif and final chord. Do not truncate the old mix.
```

The shorter version moves from Leave the day to Listen closer sooner. Off the Clock appears at 7.2 seconds, followed by the complete musical ending. No speech.

Check the markers after every recut, then export the new cue sheet. We explicitly moved the reveal and ending markers to their new frames; a shorter picture alone does not prove its scoring markers are still correct.

Finally, listen with the picture on headphones and a phone. Does the reveal feel intentional? Does the last chord have room? The timing grid can establish where a note lands. Your ears decide whether it belongs there.

**Choose the moment first, write the ending second, and fit the middle around both.** That’s the part of this workflow worth keeping, whatever tools make your next film.
