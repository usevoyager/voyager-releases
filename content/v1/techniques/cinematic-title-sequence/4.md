# Make a cinematic title sequence with moving light

Create silver lettering revealed by moving light in Voyager. Use a precise prompt, editable 3D type and a reusable lighting rig for cinematic title sequences.

Source: https://voyager.so/blog/cinematic-title-sequence

Keep the lettering simple. Let the light do the work.

That is the appeal of a restrained cinematic title: bright edges travel across the lettering, reveal its surface and disappear into black. It can give a short film, documentary chapter or launch teaser an identity without turning the opening into an effects reel.

## Make the lettering the fixed part

In Voyager, start a project, attach the reference and provide your title as text. Include the font if the identity depends on it and you have permission to use it.

For this treatment, ask for an editable Blender scene. Blender needs to be installed on the machine; Voyager can work with the scene and render tools, but does not bundle Blender itself. Keeping real text objects lets you replace the title without asking a video model to redraw the lettering.

```text
Make an eight-second cinematic title sequence inspired by the
attached reference. The exact title is INVISIBLE FORCES, set on
two lines. Keep the words editable in Blender.

Use thin, widely spaced lettering with shallow depth and small
beveled edges. The surface should feel like satin silver against
a nearly black background. Let one broad moving light reveal the
letters. Keep the camera movement very slight.

The viewer should be able to read the whole title by the middle
of the shot. Hold a readable composition at the end. No extra
words, particle bursts, random sparkles or full-frame flashes.

Show three preview frames: before the main highlight, during it
and after it. Save the scene and render an MP4 only after the
lettering and light are working together.
```

The constraints make the look reusable. Exact words stay in text objects. The material belongs to the letters. The moving light has its own animation. A change to one need not rebuild the others.

## Give silver something to reflect

A gray fill is not the same as a metallic surface. In Blender's [Principled material](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/5.0/render/shader_nodes/shader/principled.html), metallic shading reflects the environment; roughness controls how sharp that reflection appears. Small beveled edges give the light another surface to catch.

We built a separate downloadable lighting study with editable **INVISIBLE FORCES** lettering, shallow extrusion, a silver material and an animated rectangular light. A broad fill gradually lifts the ending so the words remain readable after the moving highlight passes.

INVISIBLE FORCES appears on two lines. A silver highlight travels over the fixed letters as the camera moves slightly closer. A soft fill lifts the final composition and keeps both words readable.

This version uses Fira Sans Light, shallow extrusion, a small bevel and a satin metal material. The moving strip creates the traveling reflection; a separate softbox lifts the ending. We rendered all 192 frames at 24 fps in Cycles with 96 samples and OptiX denoising, then encoded them as H.264. The download includes this MP4 and the exact editable scene used to make it.

Treat these as three separate decisions:

| Control             | What to look for                                                          |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Bevel               | A narrow bright edge that still respects the letterform                   |
| Roughness           | A highlight broad enough to read as metal, without a plastic-looking blur |
| Light size and path | A reflection that travels across the title without wiping it out          |

Start with a broad light. If the entire face turns white, change its angle or reduce its energy before adding another effect. If the title disappears, add a restrained fill rather than making every surface glow.

## Revise the highlight, not the whole shot

Look at the three preview frames together. A beautiful isolated glint is not enough if the viewer cannot read the word before it moves away.

Use a focused revision:

```text
Keep the font, wording, camera and eight-second duration.
The brightest reflection hides the letter shapes in the middle.
Broaden the moving light and reduce its energy. Keep a quieter
fill so the complete title remains readable after the sweep.
Render the same three preview frames for comparison.
```

Check the final movie at its intended viewing size. Thin strokes can vanish on a phone or break into noisy edges after compression. Review the encoded MP4, not only the Blender viewport. Keep a longer readable hold if the title is unfamiliar or contains several words.
