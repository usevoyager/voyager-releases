# Turn a place into an AI miniature-world reveal

Build an AI miniature-world reveal around a real or imagined place. Plan the opening, camera pullback and ending with reusable Voyager prompts.

Source: https://voyager.so/blog/miniature-world-reveal

The little shop is warm, detailed and easy to understand. Then the camera pulls back: it sits on a floating island, linked by bridges to a whole city in the clouds.

That change of scale is the story. **An AI miniature-world reveal works when the first place stays recognizable as the larger world appears.** It can turn a restaurant, workshop or imagined setting into an opening for a brand film, game pitch or event announcement.

A ten-second Voyager team example. The prompts below suggest a workflow for this kind of reveal.

## What you need

* **Plan:** Starter or higher, for the still and the image-to-video take. If you supply the ending frame too, use a model that accepts start and end frames.
* **Tools:** nothing beyond Voyager.

## Give the world a place to grow from

Start with a photo or a clear description of one place. Choose three details that identify it: the roofline, an illuminated window, a particular doorway or a distinctive awning. Those details matter more than asking for hundreds of buildings.

In Voyager, attach the reference and ask for a still composition first:

```text
Turn [place or business] into a miniature fantasy-world scene.
Use the reference for the building's identity: preserve [three
recognizable details]. Reinterpret the setting, not the business.

Show one beautifully detailed shop on a small floating island.
Warm light spills from the windows into a cool, misty sky.
Roots and rock extend below the island. Two distant islands hint
at a larger world, connected by delicate suspension bridges.

The opening camera is close enough to read the shop's character.
Keep its silhouette clear and its front entrance visible.
Make a 16:9 still. Do not animate yet or invent readable signage.
```

For a real business, keep any required logo as a separate asset. Generated architecture can evoke a place without reproducing every letter of its sign correctly.

## Design the destination of the camera move

A pullback needs somewhere to land. Ask for a second composition that shows how the original island fits into the larger setting:

```text
Create the ending composition for this scene. Move the camera
farther back along the same viewing axis. Keep the original shop
and island recognizable near the center-left of the frame.

Reveal a network of smaller islands, bridges and a distant
skyline. Reuse the same materials, warm window light and teal
atmosphere. Separate foreground, middle distance and background
with scale and haze. Leave clear sky between the silhouettes.
```

Compare the two images before animating. The roof, window positions, island shape and direction of light should agree. If they describe different places, fix the stills first.

This also gives you a useful design test: does the wider composition reward the viewer, or merely make the original subject too small to care about?

## Ask for a reveal, not a rebuild

Now direct the motion in the same Voyager chat:

```text
Animate the approved scene as one slow, continuous ten-second
camera pullback. Start close to the shop, then reveal the linked
islands and distant city. Keep the original architecture stable.

The world is already there; reveal it through camera movement
and clearing mist. Do not grow new walls, morph the shop or cut
to another location. Use gentle chimney smoke and small moving
lights for life. Ease into the final wide view and hold it.
```

Use image-to-video for an approved opening; if you also supply the ending, ask Voyager to select a model with start/end-frame support. Different models interpret those constraints differently, so treat them as direction rather than a guaranteed camera solve. The [MiniMax H3 guide](https://github.com/MiniMax-AI/MiniMax-H3/tree/main/skills/h3-prompt-writing) is one primary reference for describing camera movement and scene action separately.

## Keep scale cues consistent

Miniatures become convincing through comparison. A door gives the building a scale. The building gives the island a scale. The bridges make the distance between islands legible.

Watch the original shop throughout the take. Windows should not migrate, the bridge should remain attached and a foreground island should pass the camera in a believable direction. Dense fog can hide a transition, but it should not hide the entire subject.

If the geography falls apart, reduce the camera travel or the number of islands. A small, coherent world is more useful than a busy wide shot whose buildings reshape themselves.

## Turn the world into a reusable campaign asset

Once you have a good take, ask Voyager for a short cut that starts at the reveal and a still from the final wide frame. Keep the same source video for both. Add exact event or business text as editable overlays, with enough space around it to remain readable on a phone.

For a longer film, make the island view an establishing shot and cut to a closer scene inside the shop. Do not ask one clip to establish the entire city, tour its streets and tell a character story.

**Choose one place worth looking at closely, then give the viewer a reason to pull back.** The larger world should expand the idea of the place, not erase it.
