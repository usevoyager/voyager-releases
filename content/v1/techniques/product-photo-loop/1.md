# How to animate a product photo into a website loop

Animate a product photo into a subtle website video loop. Reuse our motion prompt, check the restart, and preserve the original product label.

Source: https://voyager.so/blog/product-photo-loop

A little movement in the light or background can make a product photo feel alive while keeping the packaging readable.

For this fictional **VELA citrus tonic**, we animated the leaf shadows, removed an unwanted sweep of light, trimmed a few frames to find a cleaner restart, and restored the original label over the moving footage.

## Prompt a subtle product photo animation

Start with a readable front label, a complete silhouette and room around the product. Give a curtain, reflection or leaf shadow the movement.

[Pixelcut's looping-video endpoint](https://fal.ai/models/pixelcut/looping-video/api), announced on [September 30](https://x.com/fal/status/2105337445990408287), accepts a source image and a motion prompt. We used **subtle** motion, **five seconds**, **1080p** and **no audio**.

Here is the reusable direction after our revision:

```text
Keep the camera, product, label and exposure fixed. Animate only the existing leaf shadows on the background wall: a small, slow sway out and back. Keep all illuminated areas at constant brightness. No moving beams, sweeping highlights or new objects. Return smoothly to the original shadow positions. Quiet product-page ambience.
```

Name what stays still as carefully as what moves.

## Matching endpoints is only the start

We also tried MiniMax H3 Max with the same photo supplied as both its [first and last image](https://fal.ai/models/minimax/h3-max/image-to-video/api). Both needed review.

Pixelcut introduced a bright diagonal band; H3 kept the light quieter. We revised Pixelcut to remove the band, then checked the restart.

Play at least **three consecutive cycles**. Look for:

* A jump in the shadow's position or speed when the clip restarts.
* A change in brightness, focus or texture at the join.
* Lettering that crawls or a bottle edge that changes shape.

Even the revised loop had a small reset near its endpoints. Keeping frames 7 through 116 gave us a shorter **4.58-second** loop with a cleaner join. We used a straight trim, with no crossfade or invented frames.

```sh
ffmpeg -i pixelcut-v2.mp4 \
  -vf "trim=start_frame=7:end_frame=117,setpts=PTS-STARTPTS" \
  -an -c:v libx264 -crf 18 -pix_fmt yuv420p \
  -movflags +faststart product-loop.mp4
```

For another clip, find its join by watching the motion on both sides. These frame numbers are specific to our take.

## Keep the original label

Generation kept the words **CITRUS TONIC** but redrew the lettering details.

With the camera and bottle locked, a masked composite can restore the original label over the moving footage. The download includes the script.

A rotating product or moving camera would need tracking; a fixed overlay would slide out of place.

## Make the revision in Voyager

We used a Codex agent in Voyager, our desktop app, for review and finishing. We paused on the unwanted light band and pinned this note to the frame:

```text
Remove this bright band of light. Let only the leaf shadows sway; keep the bottle and exposure steady. Make a new Pixelcut loop from source.png, preserving the original.
```

The review attachment carries the marked frame and note into the conversation. The agent makes another take from the existing photo and presents the result. Pointing at the defect supplies context the prompt alone would miss.

## Use it on a product page

[Download the source photo, prompts and finished loops](https://cdn.moda.app/voyager/blog/product-photo-loop/product-loop-kit-1c7ccd55eac0bdf7.zip). The bundle includes an HTML player that opens locally; unzip everything together so its relative video links work.

Use the MP4 with a still poster, a pause control and reduced-motion support. Keep the GIF for quick previews.

Our source is an AI-generated fictional product photo. For your own product, start with approved photography and keep exact brand details in a controlled layer. A generative prompt is a direction, not a preservation guarantee.
